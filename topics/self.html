The self

What is the self?

Also asked as: Who am I? · Who am I really? · Do I have a soul?

Traditions disagree deeply about whether a permanent self exists, but many invite us to examine what we habitually call ‘I.’

How the traditions answer

Hindu traditions

Many Vedanta schools distinguish the changing personality from Atman; Advaita identifies ultimate Atman with Brahman, while other schools preserve distinctions between soul and God.

Buddhist traditions

Anatta rejects an independent, permanent self. A person is understood through changing processes, without denying ordinary responsibility or continuity.

Abrahamic traditions

Judaism, Christianity and Islam generally understand each person as a real, morally responsible creature in relationship with God, though accounts of soul and afterlife vary.

Where they converge

All warn against reducing the person to possessions, status and passing impulses; they differ over what, if anything, remains ultimately permanent.

A question to sit with

Which parts of your identity change, and which seem continuous?
